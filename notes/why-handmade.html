Why I chose handmade furniture over mass production

By Arpan Magar · Founder, Jarghaa Furniture · Lalitpur, Nepal

When I told people I was going to build a furniture workshop by hand, the first question was always the same: why not scale? Why not import, machine it, ship it faster and cheaper?

The honest answer is that I have spent my whole career around systems that break. Networks that drop. Deals that collapse. Processes that only work while someone is watching. And in all of it, I learned the same lesson: the things that last are the things made with care.

Craft is a business decision

Handmade does not mean slow for its own sake. It means the person making your table is the same person thinking about how it will sit in your room ten years from now. That is not nostalgia — it is quality control that no spreadsheet can capture.

People pay for that. Not everyone, and not every time. But the customer who wants a piece to last, who wants to point to it and say that was made for me, will come back, and will send their friends.

What I learned from property and telecom

Running an estate agency and keeping corporate networks alive taught me to ask one question before anything else: what does the customer actually need, not what can I sell them? Mass-produced furniture answers the second question. Craft answers the first.

Build the thing you would want to keep. Everything else follows.

Building Jarghaa

Jarghaa Furniture opened in June 2025 in Lalitpur. Every piece is made to order, shaped by hand, and built to last a generation. It is slower than mass production. It is also the reason people trust it.

If you are building something in Nepal right now — anything — I would love to hear how you are thinking about quality versus speed. That conversation is the whole point of these notes.
